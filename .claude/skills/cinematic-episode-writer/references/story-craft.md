# Story Craft — writing a Bentley episode (ages 4–8)

The goal is a story so clear and felt that a small child is carried from the first beat to the last
without ever getting lost. This file is the *how*.

## The shape of an episode (~3:30–5:00)

Think of it as a tiny hero's journey. Bentley *wants* something, her *flaw* causes trouble, the
trouble gets *worse*, she *changes* (Stop. Look. Listen.), and the change *fixes* it and earns a
friend. The guest's biology drives every turn.

**Act template** (adapt beats, keep the spine):

| Act | Purpose | What happens |
|-----|---------|--------------|
| **Cold open (0:00–0:20)** | The hook | A 10–15s "wait, what?!" — a small mystery that ends on a surprise (EP2: the rock has an eyeball!). Smash to title. |
| **Act 1 — the want** | Set up Bentley + her flaw | Narrator frames Bentley + the Listening Tag. Bentley meets the mystery and tries to *catch* it (her flaw). First science fact, shown not told. |
| **Act 2 — it gets worse** | Raise the stakes | Bentley's eagerness causes real (soft) danger — the **causal hinge**: her action puts the guest at risk. End on the gut-punch realization. This act must be *shown*, never only narrated. |
| **Act 3 — the turn** | The lesson lands | Bentley **stops** — the catchphrase, the tag glows. The guest cautiously appears. We learn *why* the guest is the way they are (their fear), tied to their biology. |
| **Bonding beat** | Earn the friendship | A non-plot moment of pure joy/play between them (a color game, a shared discovery). Episodes feel hollow without this. |
| **Act 4 — the test** | Use the lesson | The danger returns; the guest is trapped; Bentley helps by **not** doing her old instinct — she coaches them to use their own power (their biology) to escape. |
| **Button (the close)** | The feeling | A warm two-shot; the emotional thesis stated simply ("you don't have to disappear to be safe with me"); a cozy final science fact over the two of them together. |
| **Cliffhanger** | Hook the next ep | The guest reveals/points to the next mystery; Bentley turns to see it; hard cut to a "NEXT TIME" card; Bentley's catchphrase as a promise. |

## The two throughlines must interlock
- **Emotional throughline:** Bentley's lesson + the friendship.
- **Educational throughline:** the guest's 3–4 real facts.
These are the **same line**, not two. Each fact should *be* a story beat: the fact explains the
danger, the escape, the fear, or the bond. If a fact is just narrated trivia with nothing depending
on it, cut it or rebuild the beat so the plot needs it. This interlock is what makes an episode feel
like a story instead of a slideshow with facts read over it.

## Causality is everything
Every beat answers "...and so?" of the beat before it. Before you finalize, walk the beats out loud:
*Bentley pounces (and so) the octopus flees (and so) toward the eel (and so) Bentley sees the danger
(and so) she finally stops...* If any "and so" is missing, the audience will feel the seam — that's
the "why are we suddenly here?" failure. Add the connective beat (often a single shot or line).

## Writing the dialogue

- **Every line reacts to the previous beat.** No line should be sayable in a different scene. A line
  that "makes no sense in context" almost always means the *setup* beat is missing, not that the line
  is bad — fix the cause.
- **Distinct voices.** Bentley: eager, impulsive, exclaims, asks blunt kind questions. Guest: quieter,
  guarded, wry. Narrator: warm wonder, gentle. You should be able to tell who's speaking with the
  labels hidden.
- **Facts as wonder, not lecture.** Narrator: "And here's a little secret — an octopus can taste by
  *touching*." Never a textbook sentence.
- **Speakable and short.** Read each line aloud. Kids' VO lines are punchy; trim adjectives. Use
  caps sparingly for the one stressed word ("You're a teeny-tiny *ME!*").
- **Earn the emotion.** The button line works only if the danger and the fear came first.
- **Catchphrase** is Bentley's, at the turn, and optionally a reprise on the close.

## Pitfalls this series has actually hit (avoid them)
- Dialogue laid in the recorded order but the *connective* lines dropped → "abrupt / out of nowhere."
  Keep the causal chain intact; restore any bridge line the plot needs.
- A guest's big speech with nothing depending on it → feels like an aside. Tie it to the danger or the
  bond.
- A "dark" line slipped in for drama → banned. Resolve fear into safety.
- Missing the bonding beat → no felt friendship at the button.
- The science "fact dump" at the end with no story weight → weave it earlier and make a beat need it.

## Worked spine (EP2, as a model)
- **Feeling:** it's safe to be seen. **Lesson:** stop and listen before you act.
- **Facts → plot:** camouflage (color+texture) = how she vanishes / why Bentley can't catch her;
  she hides *because* the eel hunts her; boneless body = how she escapes the eel through a coin-sized
  gap; taste-by-touch = the intimacy of the paw-hold; three hearts/blue blood = the cozy closing
  wonder. Every fact does plot or emotional work. That's the bar.
