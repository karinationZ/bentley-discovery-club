# Companion Short — YouTube metadata + publishing playbook

Read this for step 6 (title/description/tags) and step 7 (publishing). It carries the templates, two
worked examples (EP1, EP2), and the Made-for-Kids funnel reasoning.

## Title

Lead with a hook, keep it kid/family-friendly, append `#shorts`. Two flavors that both work:
- **Interactive** (drives comments + replays, which Shorts reward): `Can you spot the hidden octopus? 🐙 #shorts`
- **Wow/fact**: `This octopus vanishes in 1 second 🐙 #shorts`

Pick interactive when the footage has a "find it / watch it" beat; pick wow when there's a single
jaw-dropping fact. One emoji is plenty.

## Description

The **first line is the only line most people see** (everything after "…more" is hidden), so make it the
hook. Then a soft, in-character funnel to the episode (no hard-sell), then 5–7 hashtags.

Template:
```
<hook question or wow fact about the animal> <emoji> <one-sentence "how/why" payoff>.

You met <Guest> in Bentley's Big Adventures, Episode <N> — find the full cartoon adventure on our channel.

🌊 Bentley's Discovery Club — the real science behind the adventure.
Stop. Look. Listen. 🐾

#shorts #<animal> #<behavior> #oceananimals #scienceforkids #kidsscience #animalfacts
```
Notes:
- The catchphrase belongs **here** (a channel tagline in a branding context) — NOT recited in the video.
- On Made-for-Kids, description links are not clickable, so say "find it on our channel," not "click here."
- **Sanity-check auto-suggested hashtags** — they pull from channel context and can be wrong (YouTube
  suggested `#hermitcrab` on the octopus Short).

## Tags

~15 topic keywords: the animal, the specific behavior, the smart word you taught, audience terms, and the
show name. Tags are minor for ranking now — they mostly reinforce topic. Comma-separated, under 500 chars.

## Worked examples

### EP2 — octopus camouflage
- **Title:** `Can you spot the hidden octopus? 🐙 #shorts`
- **Description (first line):** `Can you spot the octopus? 🐙 An octopus can change its color AND its bumpy skin in under a second to melt into the reef — and the wildest part? It's colorblind, matching colors it can't even see!`
- **Tags:** octopus, octopus camouflage, octopus color change, chromatophores, octopus facts, ocean animals, sea creatures, under the sea, science for kids, kids science, animal facts for kids, educational, colorblind octopus, Bentleys Big Adventures

### EP1 — hermit-crab vacancy chain
- **Title:** `Hermit crabs line up to swap homes?! 🐚 #shorts`
- **Description (first line):** `Did you know hermit crabs trade homes in a line? 🐚 A hermit crab is soft, so it borrows an empty seashell — and as it grows it needs a bigger one. When a big shell washes up, a whole line of crabs swaps homes, one by one. Scientists call it a "vacancy chain"!`
- **Tags:** hermit crab, hermit crab shell, hermit crabs trading shells, vacancy chain, hermit crab facts, ocean animals, sea creatures, beach animals, tide pool, science for kids, kids science, animal facts for kids, educational, hermit crab home, Bentleys Big Adventures

## YouTube form settings
- **Video language:** English · **Category:** Education (or *Pets & Animals*) — better topical signal than
  "People & Blogs" · **Made for kids:** Yes (legally required for kids content under COPPA).

## Publishing & the funnel (Made-for-Kids reality)

Marking a video **Made for Kids** disables, by law (COPPA): **comments, clickable description links, info
cards, and end screens.** So the usual "pinned comment / clickable link / end screen" funnels do **not**
work. What works:

1. **Playlists (the main tool).** Add the Short and the full episode to **one playlist** — then the
   episode shows in the sidebar / auto-plays next and viewers can navigate to it. (Make a "Bentley's Big
   Adventures" playlist with episodes in order = the binge path.)
2. **The baked-in end-card.** This is *why* the build burns the "Find the full episode on our channel"
   thumbnail into the pixels — native end screens are off, so a visual pointer is the only option. It's
   not tappable (nothing is), so the wording says *find it on our channel*, not "tap here."
3. **Description text** still helps for search/context even though it's not clickable.

To change a video on an **already-published** Short (e.g. a new end-card), you must **delete and
re-upload** — YouTube won't swap the file on an existing upload.

## Timing (maximize the episode's reach)

- **Publish the episode first (Day 0)**, the Short **~24h later (Day 1)**. Give the long-form its own
  clean launch window (subscriber + Browse test) before the Short drives a second traffic wave — that
  second wave, once the episode has a baseline, signals "this has legs" and earns more Suggested
  placements. Same-day splits attention and points the funnel at an unvalidated video; a week+ later the
  launch window has closed.
- Best slot for kids/family: **weekend, late afternoon/early evening** local time.
- **At a small/new channel the real lever is cadence** — a steady drip of these Shorts (2–3/week) is the
  primary discovery engine, since Shorts reach cold audiences far better than long-form does.
- If the episode is already live (e.g. EP1), just publish its Short now to drive a fresh wave.
