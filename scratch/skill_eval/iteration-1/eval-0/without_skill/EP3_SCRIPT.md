# THE ANT CITY BENEATH OUR FEET
### *Bentley's Big Adventures* — Season 1 · Episode 3
**Read-through script for approval** · target runtime ~5:00

---

**CAST**
- **BENTLEY** — a young red-fawn Boxer pup. Big heart, bigger curiosity. Helps by *doing* (her flaw). *(voice: LauraZ)*
- **DOT** — a single worker ant. Tireless, anxious, certain she "doesn't matter." Wants to be enough. *(voice: locked — small, earnest, sweet)*
- **NARRATOR / "GRANDPA THEODORE"** — warm, twinkly storyteller. *(voice: Bill)*

**SETTING** — Inland from the cove, where the beach grass meets a sun-warm backyard. A crack in the dry earth opens into the hidden **Ant City** below. Late, golden afternoon — with a storm coming.

**RUNNING DEVICE** — Bentley's collar holds a small glowing gold paw-print **"Listening Tag."** When Bentley gets *still* and truly listens, it lights up. *(Stop. Look. Listen.)* **NEW THIS EPISODE:** when Bentley holds the warm shell from Indi *and* goes fully still, the tag doesn't just glow — it **shrinks her** down to ant-size for the first time.

---

## COLD OPEN — "THE CRUMB THE SIZE OF A CAR" *(0:00–0:18)*

**EXT. BEACH GRASS / DRY INLAND SAND — GOLDEN AFTERNOON.** Where Episode 2 left off. BENTLEY trots up the dune, the little spiral shell from Indi tucked under her chin, following that perfect, impossible **line of ants** across the sand — each one hauling a crumb bigger than itself. She leans down, nose almost touching the line, eyes huge.

**BENTLEY** *(whispering, awed)*
It's a *road.* A teeny-tiny road… and every single one of you is going to *work.*

*One ant at the front strains under a crumb the size of — to the ant — a **car.** Bentley's jaw drops.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)*
…You're carrying a CRUMB the size of a CAR. *(beat, delighted)* Where do you even *PARK* that?!

*She steps closer to follow — and her big paw comes down right beside the line. The earth gives a soft **crrk.** A thin crack splits open in the dry ground… leading **down** into the dark.*

**SMASH TO TITLE — *BENTLEY'S BIG ADVENTURES.***

## ACT ONE — INTO THE DARK *(0:18–1:30)*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
That's Bentley. When Bentley meets a mystery, she has one big rule: don't just *watch* it — **join** it. *(beat)* And the little glow on her collar — that's her Listening Tag. When Bentley holds still and *truly* listens, the world whispers its secrets. But today… today the tag had a brand-new trick up its sleeve. Nobody had warned her about *that* one.

*Bentley follows the ant-road right up to the crack in the earth. She presses her eye to it. Far below — a faint, warm **amber glow,** and the soft hum of… thousands. She pulls back, ears up.*

**BENTLEY**
There's a whole *down there* down there.

*She still has Indi's shell tucked under her chin. She sets it on the ground, sits, and — because she's trying so hard to be good at this now — closes her eyes to listen. The shell **warms.** The Listening Tag **glows**… brighter… then **BLAZES.**_

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(eyes still shut, small smile)*
Stop. Look. *List—*

*The world **ROARS UPWARD.** A pebble swells into a boulder. A blade of grass shoots up into a swaying redwood. The crack in the earth yawns open into a **canyon.** Bentley tumbles, paws over ears, shrinking-shrinking-shrinking until she lands — tiny — on a grain of sand the size of a beanbag.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(spinning, panicked, thrilled)*
Why is everything getting so— so— **BIG?!**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Ohh, no, no, little one. Everything didn't get bigger. *(warm chuckle)* *You* got smaller. Welcome… to the city beneath your feet.

*A shadow falls over her. Bentley looks up — and **UP.** A single **ANT** stands over her like a glossy six-legged bus, antennae sweeping. Bentley's tail tucks, then — being Bentley — un-tucks. She grins.*

**BENTLEY**
…Okay. *Best.* Tuesday. *Ever.*

---

## ACT TWO — CAPTAIN BENTLEY *(1:30–2:30)*

*The giant ant — **DOT** — doesn't even slow down. She's hauling a seed three times her size, marching a dead-straight invisible line, antennae brushing the ground every step. Bentley scrambles alongside, ant-sized now, full of helpful energy.*

**BENTLEY**
Hi! Hi. I'm Bentley. That looks SO heavy, let me— here, I'll show you a faster way! Shortcut! This way's way shorter, see, straight to the—

*She bounds ahead and plants herself in the lead like a tiny general.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(booming, paws on hips)*
Okay, everyone! New plan! **FOLLOW ME!**

*Behind her, a whole **column of ants** flows past — completely ignoring her — following a path she can't even see. Bentley deflates, then re-inflates.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)*
…Follow me? *(beat)* Anybody? Hello? I'm RIGHT here, I'm the captain now—

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Here's the thing about an ant, little friends. One ant, all alone, can't do very much at all. But ants almost *never* are alone. A colony is what we call a **superorganism** — thousands of tiny bodies, thinking and working like one enormous animal. They farm. They build. They out-engineer almost anything. And not one of them is waiting around for a captain.

*Bentley, determined to *help,* spots the ants all following one invisible track in the dirt. She squints at it, doesn't get it — and decides the line is just *messy.* She sweeps her paw across it to "tidy it up."*

**BENTLEY** *(brightly, scrubbing the trail away)*
There. Cleaned that right up for you. Nice straight— oh. Oh no.

*The instant her paw smears the path, the marching column **breaks.** Ants stop dead. Antennae wave, confused. The neat road dissolves into milling, lost little dots. Dot freezes, seed still on her back, suddenly facing nowhere.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(horrified whisper)*
…What did I just do?

---

## ACT THREE — THE TURN *(2:30–3:25)*

*Far above, through the canyon-crack, the golden light snuffs out. A low **rumble.** A fat drop of rain hits the sand near Bentley like a water balloon. Then another. The storm has arrived — and the ants are scattered, blind, off their road.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)** *(gentle, urgent)*
That invisible road wasn't messy, Bentley. It was the most important thing in the whole city. And you just wiped it away — right as the rain came.

*Bentley looks at her own paw, then at the lost, swirling ants, then up at the gathering dark. Her ears fall. She makes herself **stop.** Sits. Closes her eyes. And this time she means it.*

**BENTLEY** *(quiet; her catchphrase)*
Stop. Look. *Listen.*

*The Listening Tag glows soft and warm. Bentley does what a dog does best — she lowers her nose all the way to the ground and **breathes in.** Her eyes snap open: she can *smell* it now — a faint, glowing, scent-line shimmering across the dirt, the ghost of the road.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(wondering)*
…It's not a road you *see.* It's a road you *smell.*

*And in the hush, a small voice — Dot's — drifts up, talking to herself, near tears.*

**DOT** *(small, defeated)*
I'm just one ant. I can't fix this. I never could. There's a *million* of us — nobody would even notice if I just… stopped.

*Bentley turns. For the first time she really **looks** at the one ant in front of her.*

---

## ACT THREE-AND-A-HALF — TALKING BY SMELL *(the bonding beat) (3:25–3:55)*

*Bentley pads over, soft and careful now, and lies down nose-to-antenna with Dot — small to small.*

**BENTLEY** *(gentle)*
Hey. Hi. I'm the one who wrecked your road. I'm… really sorry. *(beat)* Can you show me how it works? The smell-road?

*Dot hesitates — then, shy, reaches out an antenna and taps a fresh dot of scent onto the ground. A tiny spark of amber light blooms where she touches.*

**DOT**
Like… like this. We don't shout. We can't shout. We leave a smell — a little dab — every few steps. *(small pride creeping in)* And then everyone behind you knows exactly where the food is. Or where it's safe.

*Bentley watches Dot lay three glowing dots in a perfect line. Her tail does one slow, wondering wag.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
That's their secret, little ones. Ants talk by **smell.** They lay an invisible trail — a scent called a *pheromone* — drop by drop, so the whole colony can follow the same path to food, or water, or home. A million tiny voices… all saying the same thing without a single sound.

**BENTLEY** *(soft, to Dot)*
You don't shout… and the whole city still hears you. *(beat)* That's the opposite of "doesn't matter."

---

## ACT FOUR — THE FLOOD *(3:55–4:30)*

*KRA-KOOM. The rain comes hard now. Water sheets down the canyon walls and begins to **pour into the city.** Through a gap, Bentley sees it — the low chamber where the colony keeps its tiny, pearl-white **eggs,** filling fast. Lost ants mill helplessly on the wrong side of a widening puddle.*

*Bentley's instinct fires. She throws her whole tiny body against the flood — paws scooping, trying to dam it alone.*

**BENTLEY** *(straining)*
I've got it— I'll block it— one dog, one wall, I can— !

*The water shoves her flat on her back. One dog. One paw. It's not enough. She lies there, soaked, beaten.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(small)*
…I can't do it by myself.

*A beat. Then she looks at Dot. At the scattered ants. At the road she now knows how to fix.*

**BENTLEY** *(CONT'D)* *(igniting)*
But I don't *have* to. Dot— how strong is one ant? Really?

**DOT** *(blinking)*
We can lift fifty times our own weight. And we can— *(realizing)* —we can hold *onto each other.*

**BENTLEY**
Then let's build the thing ANTS build. Not my way. *Yours.*

*And it happens — gorgeous, fast: ant after ant **links legs,** body to body, weaving a **living bridge** across the flood, then a **living raft** that floats right on the rising water. They begin ferrying the eggs, one pearl at a time, up to high ground.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)** *(marveling)*
And this is the truest thing of all. An ant can lift fifty times its weight — but link them together, leg to leg, and they become something new entirely. A **bridge** made of bodies. A **raft** that floats a whole city across the water. No ant could do it. *Every* ant can.

*Bentley races the failing scent-road, dabbing fresh glowing dots from the warm shell — re-laying the path so the ferrying ants don't get lost — while Dot stands at the front of the raft, calling the others on.*

---

## ACT FIVE — THE ONE THE MILLION NEEDED *(4:30–4:50)*

*The last egg lands safe on high, dry ground. The flood crests… and falls. The city holds. Exhausted ants pile together, safe. Dot stands at the center of it, stunned at what she did.*

**DOT** *(quiet)*
There are a *million* of us. Nobody would even notice if I was gone.

**BENTLEY** *(warm, certain)*
Okay — but who found the broken road first?

**DOT** *(small)*
…I did.

**BENTLEY**
And who just led everybody across that bridge?

**DOT** *(growing)*
…I did.

**BENTLEY** *(grinning)*
See? Small isn't the same as "doesn't matter." Trust me — I'm currently the size of a *crumb.* *(beat, gentle)* You're not just one ant, Dot. You're one of a million. And tonight… the million needed the **one.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)** *(tender)*
A whole city, saved — not by the biggest, or the loudest. By a million small ones who held on tight to each other… and by the one brave enough to lead.

## TAG / CLIFFHANGER → EPISODE 4 *(4:50–5:00)*

*The rain clears. Sun breaks through the canyon-crack in a single golden shaft. Down it, soft as a falling leaf, drifts a **monarch butterfly** — vast and stained-glass-bright to tiny Bentley — and lands trembling on the dirt beside her. Up close, its wings shiver with nerves.*

**MONARCH** *(quiet, scared)*
I'm leaving today. On the Big Journey. *(beat)* And I'm… I'm not ready.

*The Listening Tag glows — and tips, like a compass, straight **up** toward the open sky. Bentley looks up the shaft of light, eyes huge.*

**BENTLEY** *(quiet, to herself — her promise; catchphrase)*
Stop. Look. *Listen.*

*A forager ant nearby murmurs, almost to no one: "…and the flowers by the sea are closing up." Bentley's ears flick toward it — another piece of the cove's big secret.*

**HARD CUT TO CARD: "NEXT TIME: THE BUTTERFLY HIGHWAY."**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
She came down to find a city. She's leaving with a *map to the sky.*

### END OF EPISODE

*Science checked (ants): a colony is a **superorganism** — one ant is nearly helpless; together they farm, build, and "think" as a group. Ants communicate chemically via **pheromone trails** (scent laid drop-by-drop, followed by the colony to food/safety) — not by sound. Many ants can carry **~50× their body weight**, and species link bodies into **living bridges and floating rafts** to cross gaps and survive floods. Workers are sterile females; brood ("eggs/pearls") are tended in chambers — kept simple/visual for ages 4–8. Storm-flood + raft behavior is real (e.g. fire ants raft); dramatized gently. Season thread breadcrumb: "the flowers by the sea are closing up" (links to bees/flowers/ocean later).*
