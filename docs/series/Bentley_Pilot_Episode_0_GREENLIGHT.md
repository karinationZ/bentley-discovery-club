# Bentley's Discovery Club
# PILOT — Episode 0: "The Voice in the Shell" (GREENLIGHT DRAFT)

> **Logline:** When a tiny voice cries for help from a glowing shell, Bentley the Boxer charges in to save the day the way she does everything — fast, loud, and wrong — until a one-armed sea star teaches her that the world only tells you what it needs when you *stop, look, and listen.*

**Runtime target:** 7–8 min (with a tested 5-min cut — see end).
**Primary business goal of this episode:** Make the audience fall in love with Bentley AND teach the channel's repeatable promise: *every episode, a real mystery in nature, solved by listening.*
**Bentley's arc:** Helps by *doing* → fails → learns to help by *listening*. (This is the engine of the whole series.)

# ACT 1 — THE FLAW ON DISPLAY (0:18–1:45)

WIDE — finally — the **Crystal Cove tide pools** at early morning, Orange County. Tide is *going out*. Golden light, glassy water, rocks slick and shining. It's beautiful — but we hold it for two seconds, no more.

> **NARRATOR (warm, like a favorite aunt):** "This is Bentley. Bentley loves the beach. Bentley loves snacks—"

Bentley pulls a **dog biscuit** from her collar pouch, considers it, tucks it back. "Emergency snack. For later."

> **NARRATOR:** "—but most of all, Bentley loves to *help.* The only problem... is *how.*"

Bentley scrambles back to the glowing shell. The voice again:

> **PIP (O.S., from a crack in the high rocks):** "Down here! On the dry rock!"

Bentley spots him: **PIP** — a thumb-sized **hermit crab**, jammed in a too-tight shell, stranded on a sun-baked rock *above* the waterline. Grumpy, tough, tiny-dog energy.

> **BENTLEY:** *(gasp)* "A talking crab! Don't worry, tiny guy — I am an *expert* helper. Obviously you fell out of the ocean!"
> **PIP:** "What? No—"
> **BENTLEY:** "So I'll just put you BACK!"

She scoops Pip in her mouth and **gallops toward the open surf** — big crashing waves.

> **PIP:** "NO no no not the BIG water — I'm too little for the big water! Put me DOWN, you enormous—"

A wave nearly swallows them. Bentley backpedals, skids, **drops Pip** on the sand. He's fine — but rattled.

> **PIP:** *(furious, pointing a claw)* "You almost fed me to the *ocean!*"
> **BENTLEY:** *(crushed)* "I was *helping*..."

⚡ **[SHORTS CLIP #2 — "Bentley's Wrong Guesses: the worst rescue ever"]**

**Open loop planted:** if the ocean's wrong, what does Pip actually need? Kids lean in.

# ACT 2A — DISCOVERY 1 & 2, AND IT GETS WORSE (1:45–3:15)

A calm voice drifts up from a sunny tide pool.

> **STELLA (O.S.):** "He's not from the ocean, sweetheart. He's from *here.*"

Reveal **STELLA** — a violet **sea star** draped on a rock. One of her five arms is short — visibly *regrowing*, a smaller bud. She's unbothered, wise, a little funny.

> **BENTLEY:** "Whoa — what happened to your arm?"
> **STELLA:** "Lost it. Growing it back. Sea stars do that." *(stretches the little bud)* "Takes time. Most good things do."

🟢 **[FACT 1 — dramatized, not lectured: sea stars regrow lost arms.]**

> **STELLA:** "These little pools? They're the ocean's *leftovers.* When the tide goes out, it forgets a few puddles behind. Everyone here knows the rules. Pip just woke up too high, too dry, and now—" *(she glances at the climbing sun)* "—now it's a race."

🟢 **[FACT 2: tide pools form when the tide retreats — and this is the CLOCK, not a footnote. As the sun rises, the high rocks heat up and a stranded tide-pool animal can dry out.]**

Bentley, determined to fix it FAST, spots a big beautiful **"flower"** on a rock. "A flower! I'll give Pip a comfy flower bed!" She nudges her emergency biscuit toward it as a gift—

The "flower" — a **sea anemone** — *closes around the biscuit and swallows it.* Bentley shrieks and does panicked **zoomies**, knocking a tiny pebble-slide toward Pip.

> **STELLA:** *(dry)* "That's not a flower. That's an *animal.* Anemones look like plants. They are *not* plants. And they *will* eat your snack."

🟢 **[FACT 3: sea anemones are animals, not plants.]** ⚡ **[SHORTS CLIP #3 — "I fed my snack to a FLOWER (it was not a flower)"]**

The pebble-slide knocks Pip into an even *worse* spot — wedged, shell scraping, the sun now blazing. **Stakes up. Her rushing made it worse.** Pip's voice goes small and scared.

> **PIP:** "...I'm really stuck now."

---

# ACT 2B — THE TURN: STOP. LOOK. LISTEN. (3:15–4:30)

Bentley spirals — paces, whimpers, reaches to grab him again—

> **STELLA:** "Bentley. *Stop.*"

Everything goes quiet. The music drops out. Just waves and breath.

> **STELLA:** "You keep *doing.* You haven't *listened.* The world's been telling you what it needs this whole time. Try it. **Stop. Look. Listen.**"

Bentley sits. Closes her eyes. Takes a breath. For the first time, she's *still.*

**The shell on her collar GLOWS — bright, warm, alive.** The soundscape blooms: she can suddenly *hear everything* — the pool, the crabs, Pip's actual small voice underneath the panic.

> **PIP:** *(quiet, real)* "...I'm not scared of being stuck. I'm scared 'cause I'm *growing.* My shell got too tight and nothing fits and everybody's too busy to help and I thought... I'd just have to stay too small forever."

Beat. Bentley's eyes well up. *This* she understands.

> **BENTLEY:** *(gently, finally)* "You're not too small. You're just between shells. ...I know that feeling."

🟢 **[FACT 4 setup: a hermit crab's shell isn't its body — it's a borrowed *house*, and crabs must swap to bigger empty shells as they grow.]**

⚡ **[SHORTS CLIP #4 — the emotional turn: "I'm scared because I'm growing." / "You're not too small. You're just between shells."]**

This is the Bluey beat. *Outgrowing things and feeling unheard* — every kid and every parent knows it.

---

# ACT 3 — CLIMAX: THE VACANCY CHAIN + THE CLOCK (4:30–6:00)

Now Bentley *helps the right way* — by understanding the real need. She doesn't carry Pip. She organizes the neighborhood.

> **BENTLEY:** "You need a bigger house. And I bet somebody here is moving up too. Discovery Club — *let's GO!*"

She rallies the tide pool. Turns out hermit crabs do something amazing and *real*:

> **STELLA:** "When one crab finds a bigger shell, the next-biggest moves into *its* old one, and the next, and the next. We line up by size. We call it the *vacancy chain.*"
> **PIP:** *(muttering)* "Location, location, tide-*pool*-cation."

🟢 **[FACT 5 — the climax mechanic: hermit crabs form a "vacancy chain," lining up by size to swap shells in sequence. 100% real, and it's the SET PIECE.]** *(Parent-level joke lands here: the crustacean housing market.)*

The crabs line up biggest-to-smallest. A big crab finds a roomy empty whelk shell — *click* — moves in, leaves its old one — *click, click, click* down the line — a perfect domino of homes.

**SETBACK (the required failure):** The chain reaches Pip — but the last empty shell is **one size too small.** It won't fit. The sun is *brutal* now; the rock is steaming; Pip is fading. The chain's broken. Everyone freezes.

> **BENTLEY:** *(almost rushes... then stops herself)* "...Okay. Stop. Look. *Listen.*"

She gets still again. Looks — *really* looks. And sees it: her own **collar shell** — the glowing one from the cold open — is *exactly* Pip's size. Empty. Perfect.

> **BENTLEY:** "It was on me the whole time."

She sets the shell down. Pip slips in. **Perfect fit.** The shell glows once — *home.* The whole pool cheers as a fresh wave finally rolls back in and carries Pip safe into the cool water — *beating the clock by a breath.*

⚡ **[SHORTS CLIP #5 — the vacancy-chain domino + the perfect-fit payoff: "watch REAL hermit crabs trade houses."]**

# EMOTIONAL BUTTON + LESSON (6:00–7:00)

Pip, safe in the water, in his glowing new home, looks up at Bentley.

> **PIP:** "You gave me your shell."
> **BENTLEY:** "You needed it more. Besides — turns out I don't help by *grabbing.* I help by *listening.*"
> **PIP:** *(quietly)* "...You're the first one who actually did."

Stella floats over, presses the now-empty *first* shell — no wait — she produces a *new* tiny shell and clips it to Bentley's collar. It glows.

> **STELLA:** "Every good listener gets one. It glows when somebody nearby needs help — and it only works when you get *quiet* enough to hear them. Welcome to the Discovery Club, Bentley."

> **NARRATOR:** "Bentley came to the beach to *help.* She left knowing the secret: the whole world is talking, all the time. You just have to get quiet enough to hear it."

> **BENTLEY:** *(to camera, warm)* "Stop. Look. Listen. ...Discover."

## WHAT CHANGED FROM THE ORIGINAL (the greenlight fixes)

1. **One spine, not two.** The cold-open "Help!" *is* the climax problem (Pip). Inciting incident and climax are now the same thread.
2. **Real arc.** Bentley starts helping by *doing* (and making it worse), learns to *listen*, solves by understanding. Earns the "you finally started listening" line — now it's the whole story, not a throwaway.
3. **Stakes + clock + setback.** The rising sun is a ticking clock; the too-small shell is the required failure before the win. Jeopardy without scariness.
4. **Science dramatized, not listed.** All five facts are load-bearing plot, not a bullet block. The vacancy chain *is* the climax. Stella's regrowing arm *is* the theme ("good things take time").
5. **Comedy actually written.** Wrong-rescue, the snack-eating "flower," zoomies, the crustacean housing-market gag for parents.
6. **Hook at 0:00.** Opens on the talking shell, not the sunrise. Scenery held to 2 seconds, after the hook.
7. **Magic system that means something.** The shell glows *only when she listens* — the power IS the lesson. Renewable across every episode and a hero merch item.
8. **Channel hooks built in.** Branded sting, rally cry, catchphrase, CTA, and 5 pre-tagged Shorts.
