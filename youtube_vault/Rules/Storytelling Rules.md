# Storytelling Rules

> [!important] Entertain first, teach second
> Every episode must **teach one clear concept** — but it must be **entertaining first, educational second**, and **end on a memorable takeaway**. If it doesn't work as a *story*, the lesson won't land.

## The five-beat structure
> [!example] The arc every episode follows
> 1. **Introduction** — meet Bentley in Crystal Cove; establish wonder and a hook.
> 2. **Setup** — Bentley meets the guest creature; we learn who they are.
> 3. **Problem** — the creature has a real problem, rooted in the episode's science.
> 4. **Climax** — the problem peaks; the science is revealed as the key.
> 5. **Resolution** — the problem is solved *using* the concept; the warmth pays off.
>
> ➕ **Educational takeaway** — one line a child can repeat.

## Every episode must
- [ ] Teach **one** clear concept (not three) — see [[National Geographic Standards]]
- [ ] Be **entertaining first**, educational second
- [ ] End on a **memorable takeaway** a 5-year-old can repeat
- [ ] Give the science a **feeling** — attach it to a character's problem
- [ ] End on **Bentley's face** (the star), *then* any cliffhanger button

## Craft notes
- **Open a loop, pay it off.** Plant a small mystery or gag early, resolve it late. *(Ep 1: Bentley's "are you a tiny knight?" mis-guess pays off at the end.)* Keeps older kids engaged.
- **Two spines, braided.** The **emotional** spine (a life lesson) and the **science** spine (the concept) should resolve **together**.
- **Stinger button.** End with a light "new friend" tease for next episode — *after* Bentley's resolved last face, never instead of it.
- **Length:** ~4–5 minutes.
